A study by Apple researchers, titled “The Super Weight in Large Language Models ,” demonstrates that an extremely minuscule portion of parameters—sometimes a single scalar weight—can exercise critical leverage over an entire Large Language Model's generation quality. The investigation details the nature of these singular “super weights” alongside their induced “super activations,” presenting new perspectives on Transformer internal dynamics and model compression techniques. Complete empirical datasets and methodology are detailed in the paper , while this summary outlines the foundational findings.

Understanding and Compressing Increasingly Large Models

Although modern LLMs achieve remarkable capabilities across diverse language tasks, their vast parameter counts—often reaching tens or hundreds of billions—pose substantial deployment hurdles for edge hardware and mobile devices. Minimizing the compute footprint and memory bandwidth requirements of LLMs is vital for local, private, and offline execution. However, effective optimization requires deep architectural understanding, as unguided parameter quantization or pruning can severely degrade model output quality.

Identifying Super Weights and Their Impact

Previous studies established that small subsets of outlier weights (roughly 0.01% of parameters) play vital roles in preserving model performance during compression. In billion-parameter architectures, however, 0.01% still corresponds to hundreds of thousands of individual values. Apple researchers identified that an even far more extreme subset—isolated single scalars termed “super weights”—can single-handedly determine model viability. In Llama-7B, zeroing out a single super weight value causes perplexity to jump by orders of magnitude while zero-shot performance collapses to random guessing, whereas zeroing thousands of other outlier parameters yields negligible degradation.

The paper introduces an efficient methodology to isolate super weights through a single forward pass. This technique detects “super activations”—unusually high-magnitude activation outliers caused directly by super weights. These activations persist across subsequent Transformer layers via residual skip connections with static positions and magnitudes regardless of input prompt variation. Super weights consistently localize within the down-projection layer of Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs) immediately following attention modules in early network layers.

As indexed in Table 1, super weights manifest within specific linear projection matrices early in the Transformer pipeline across diverse model families. The generated super activation travels through residual skip connections, as visualized in Figure 2 below. This sustained high-magnitude activation suppresses high-probability non-informative stopwords in the output logit distribution. When the super weight is pruned, this suppression vanishes, driving up stopword probabilities and suppressing meaningful content tokens.

Enhanced Compression and Model Understanding

Recognizing super weights and their induced activation spikes yields immediate practical gains for post-training quantization (PTQ) and model compression. Because key output dynamics depend heavily on these isolated parameters, isolating and preserving them with high numerical precision allows simple round-to-nearest (RTN) quantization to achieve performance on par with complex state-of-the-art compression frameworks.

Specifically, keeping super weights intact while clipping remaining outlier weights enables effective RTN quantization across substantially larger block sizes, significantly improving compression ratios. This targeted, hardware-friendly strategy avoids the computational overhead of managing hundreds of thousands of generic outliers, facilitating high-quality LLM execution on constrained mobile device hardware.

Exploring the Landscape of Super Outliers

These findings highlight promising avenues for future research into Transformer optimization and interpretability. Further study into how super weights emerge during pre-training could inform more resilient optimization algorithms and architectural designs. Broadening this analysis across diverse multimodal architectures and training objectives will help researchers build more compact, robust, and interpretable language models.
